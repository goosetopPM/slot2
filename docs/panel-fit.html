Panel Fit

Every panel SLOT2 ships for, against every console it runs. Integer takes the largest whole multiple that fits and ignores the console's intended shape; AspectFit takes the largest size that keeps that shape and accepts a fractional factor. The arithmetic below is place() and Aspect::display(), transcribed.

Every combination at once. Orange is the integer factor; teal is the fractional one AspectFit would use, and how far the integer picture sits from the shape the console meant.

Why Integer is the default

Every source pixel becomes the same number of screen pixels, so sprite edges stay hard and 8×8 text stays legible. A fractional factor means filtering, which on a 240-pixel-wide picture is the difference between reading a menu and squinting at it.

Why the shapes are wrong

A NES pixel is 8:7 — 256×240 was meant to look wider than it measures. Integer keeps the buffer's proportions, so the picture comes out 12.5% narrow. Nobody who grew up on a CRT will mistake it; nobody playing on a handheld tends to mind.

Why the Mega Drive is modelled differently

It switches between 256 and 320 pixels across mid-scene and both filled the same 4:3 screen, so there is no constant pixel ratio to store. Aspect::Display holds the shape fixed instead, which is what stops the picture jumping when a game changes mode.

720×720 is the odd one

The tall panel is the only one that buys a Game Boy a fourth step of scale, and the only one where a 4:3 console leaves a band of unused screen above and below rather than at the sides.
